# Market Localization Playbook: DC, Maryland, Virginia, Delaware

This document explains *why* each jurisdiction's pages say what they say — the regulatory and housing-stock research that drives the localized copy. It also serves as the checklist for writing any future market's content.

## Why jurisdiction-level localization is the moat

Any competitor can template "We buy houses in {city}." Almost none of them explain what TOPA does to a tenanted DC sale or why a Virginia seller in default has weeks where a Maryland seller has months. That knowledge:

- ranks (unique, substantive content that satisfies query intent),
- converts (sellers instantly recognize whether a buyer knows their market),
- qualifies (a seller who reads the foreclosure-timeline explanation arrives at the call already understanding urgency and process).

## Washington, DC

**Housing stock:** Victorian/early-1900s rowhouses, party walls, historic districts, basement units. High share of long-held family property east of the Anacostia River.

**Regulatory angles used on pages:**
- **TOPA (Tenant Opportunity to Purchase Act):** tenanted sales require statutory notice and, for multi-unit buildings, formal offers of sale. This is the single biggest differentiator between DC and everywhere else — it anchors the DC state page and the tired-landlord situation page.
- **Historic districts / HPRB:** exterior repairs on much of Capitol Hill need review-board approval, which deters retail fixer-upper buyers — an as-is cash buyer's advantage, so the Capitol Hill page leads with it.
- **Heirs' property / tangled title:** prevalent in Wards 7–8; the Anacostia page addresses probate in DC Superior Court and funding title-clearing work.
- **Unlicensed basement units:** common in Petworth; a licensing headache in a retail sale, a non-issue in a direct sale.

**Markets:** Capitol Hill, Anacostia, Petworth, Brookland (neighborhood-level, since DC has no sub-cities).

## Maryland

**Housing stock:** Baltimore's enormous rowhome inventory (including vacants), post-war inner-Beltway suburbs in PG County, Montgomery County's premium-but-aging stock, 2000s-boom commuter subdivisions in Charles County, steel-era housing in Dundalk.

**Regulatory angles used on pages:**
- **Ground rent:** a Baltimore-specific leasehold arrangement dating to the colonial era; redemption runs through the state (SDAT). Unfamiliar to out-of-area buyers, and a real closing complication — the Baltimore page owns this topic.
- **Foreclosure timeline:** no filing before 120 days of delinquency; Order to Docket in circuit court; pre-file mediation available. This "you have more time than you think" framing anchors the MD regulation callout.
- **PG County rental licensing:** inspection/licensing regime that burdens small landlords — Hyattsville page angle.
- **County transfer/recordation tax stacking:** Montgomery County closing-math surprise — Silver Spring page angle.
- **FEMA flood-zone remapping:** kills financed deals near the water — Dundalk/Edgemere angle.

**Markets:** Baltimore, Silver Spring, Hyattsville, Waldorf, Dundalk.

## Virginia

**Housing stock:** high-land-value teardowns in Arlington, aging 1970s–80s Fairfax subdivisions, historic Richmond rowhouses with vacant-registry exposure, flood-exposed Norfolk bungalows, boom-era Prince William subdivisions.

**Regulatory angles used on pages:**
- **Non-judicial foreclosure:** the region's fastest — trustee sale possible with brief published notice after default. Urgency framing drives the VA state page and the Woodbridge page (trustee relationships, pre-auction closings).
- **Military PCS cycles:** the Pentagon (Arlington) and Naval Station Norfolk make orders-driven sales a structural market; both pages cover report-date closings, remote signing, and VA-loan entitlement restoration.
- **HOA lien arrears:** stall conventional closings in planned Fairfax subdivisions — resolved from proceeds at settlement.
- **Richmond vacant building registry:** escalating fees on empty property; a direct sale stops the meter.

**Markets:** Arlington, Fairfax, Richmond, Norfolk, Woodbridge.

## Delaware

**Housing stock:** Wilmington urban rowhomes (with a vacant-property fee regime), Newark student rentals, Dover base-adjacent and manufactured housing, rural well/septic properties around Milford, beach-area cottages and leasehold-land communities in Sussex.

**Regulatory angles used on pages:**
- **Judicial foreclosure + mediation:** lenders must sue in Superior Court; the state's mediation program precedes judgment. The longest runway in the region — the DE callout frames it as usable time.
- **Wilmington vacant property registration:** annually escalating fees — the Wilmington page's core angle.
- **Sussex septic transfer inspections:** a failed system is a five-figure obstacle to any financed sale — Milford's angle, with DNREC permitting handled post-close.
- **Leasehold land (Long Neck etc.):** homes on rented ground that lenders won't finance on resale — Rehoboth's angle, since cash is often the only exit.
- **Manufactured-home financing gaps:** shrinks Dover-area sellers' buyer pool to cash.

**Markets:** Wilmington, Newark, Dover, Milford, Rehoboth Beach.

## Checklist for adding a new market

Write all six fields before the page ships — if you can't, the market isn't researched enough to publish:

1. `intro` — what the housing stock is and which seller situations dominate here (3–4 sentences, no reusable phrasing).
2. `localAngle` — one jurisdiction-specific rule, tax, program, or market quirk, explained concretely, with how we absorb it.
3. `neighborhoods` — 4–5 real sub-areas locals would use.
4. `landmark` — one natural local entity mention.
5. `zips` — the actual zip codes, verified; these drive lead routing.
6. `testimonial` — a review whose substance references the `localAngle` (this is what makes it credible).

Plus: `medianDaysToClose` (realistic per market), `nearby` links (2 same-state markets), and lat/lng for the schema geo-coordinates.

## Compliance notes for production

- Regulation summaries (TOPA, foreclosure timelines, mediation programs) must be attorney-reviewed per jurisdiction before launch and re-reviewed annually — statutes change.
- Never give legal advice on-page; every regulatory mention frames it as "how we handle it," with the footer disclaimer directing users to licensed professionals.
- Testimonials in production must be real and verifiable (the ones in this demo are illustrative placeholders, disclosed in the footer).
- Advertised timelines ("close in 14 days") must reflect actual operational medians per market.
